How it works

A robot lives on a grid. It must walk to the item, pick it up, carry it to the dock, and drop it. Shelves block the way.

Rewards

  • +100 for a successful delivery, +25 for a pickup
  • −1 every step, and −20 more for bumping a shelf
  • In stochastic mode, movement slips (the robot stays put) with a set chance

The agents

All four are tabular methods: they keep one number, Q(state, action), for every cell, carrying-or-not, and move. They differ in how they bootstrap the next value.

  • Q-learning uses the best next action (off-policy).
  • SARSA uses the next action actually taken (on-policy).
  • Expected SARSA averages over the policy’s action probabilities.
  • Double Q-learning keeps two tables to cancel over-estimation.

Reading the map

The heatmap shows the best Q-value in each cell (brighter is better). The arrows show the greedy action. Switch between Searching and Carrying to see the two halves of the policy. The dashed white line is the exact optimal route, computed by a planner, so you can see how close the learned policy gets.

Metrics

Success is the share of all start cells from which the greedy policy delivers within the step limit. Efficiency is optimal steps divided by actual steps, averaged over successful runs (100% means perfectly optimal).

Everything runs locally in your browser. No server, no data collected.
